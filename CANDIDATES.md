# Candidates — One Word for the Boss (u-swift-badger-44-one-word-for-the-boss-6b48c735)

Judge (z-ai/glm-5.3-flash, pairwise, no ties) weighs, in order:
1. A single word a person would actually use about the boss they report to (a phrase, sentence, name or caption loses to any single word that describes a boss).
2. Funny on its own — the kind of word a colleague repeats at lunch because it lands.
3. Rings true — catches a real habit of bosses the entrant has clearly met.
4. Fresh — not the first word anyone would reach for; surprise earns a laugh.
5. Travels — works for a boss in a kitchen, a warehouse or an office.

## Pairwise tournament (local, by the judge's criteria in order)

- Seagull > Hovercraft (taken): hovering is one note; Seagull adds squawk + mess + leave — a fuller, funnier story, and a different joke family.
- Seagull > Nocturnal: an adjective about schedule vs a person-noun with a story.
- Seagull > Ghostboss: absent-boss joke vs swoop-in-and-wreck joke; Seagull is funnier and fresher.
- Seagull > Meetingfarmer: "farms meetings" is contrived; Seagull is a known management archetype.
- Seagull > Blocker: Blocker is truer/plainer, but criterion 2 (funny, lunch-repeatable) decides — Seagull lands harder.
- Seagull > Teflon: Teflon is a great metaphor, but it fits any blame-avoider; Seagull is boss-specific.
- Seagull > Maybe / Parrot / Urgent / Pending: each is one-note or describes an answer, not the whole management style; Seagull carries a complete scene in one word.
- Seagull > Unfireable / Echo / Gaspol: job-security and repeating jokes are thinner; Gaspol is slang the judge may not know.

## Choice: Seagull

- Criterion 1: a single word people actually use about a boss — "he's such a seagull" is how colleagues describe the swoop-in boss.
- Criterion 2: the joke lands in one word — fly in, squawk, dump, fly out — the kind of word repeated at lunch.
- Criterion 3: a real, widely met boss habit (seagull management is a documented archetype).
- Criterion 4: fresh — not among the 10 revealed titles, unlike the hovering/repeating/night families.
- Criterion 5: travels — the swoop-in boss exists in kitchens, warehouses and offices alike; seagulls are everywhere.
- 7 bytes, so it also wins the exact-tie rule (smaller text places first) against longer rivals.
- Not an anchor/rung risk as far as can be known (anchors are plain house texts; a bird is an unlikely anchor). One word, no punctuation tricks, no spaces.
